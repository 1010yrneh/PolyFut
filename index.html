Mark out the pitch

Optional, about a minute. Without it the app judges “who touched the ball” in pixels, which stretches to ~50 m at the far side of the pitch — the main reason other players show up in your hotspots. Marking the pitch fixes that.

Pick a landmark, then click exactly where it is. The yellow pitch is what your clicks imply — it should sit on the real painted lines.

Add 4 or more landmarks to begin. Aim for 8, spread near and far — points all along one line cannot be solved.

Why can this take a while?

  • Analysis runs entirely on your computer — every frame is decoded and scanned locally, not in the cloud.
  • Long matches are processed in 30-second chunks; a 90-minute game can mean ~180+ chunks of detect-and-track work.
  • The engine looks for players and the ball on thousands of sampled frames so distant touches are not missed.
  • On a typical laptop CPU, a full match often needs several hours. Slow progress (e.g. chunk 40/189) is expected, not a freeze.

Tip: You can turn off the display or lock the screen — analysis keeps running on the server. You can also close this tab and tap Resume later. Only a full computer shutdown or sleep will stop the run. Keep your laptop plugged in.

Personalize PolyFut

AI scout reports already work — there is nothing to set up. Skip this and start analysing.

The AI allowance is shared between everyone using PolyFut, so it can occasionally be busy. Adding your own free Groq key takes about a minute and gives you your own allowance to fall back on.

  1. Open the Groq API Console and sign in (Google / GitHub / email).
  2. Click Create API Key, give it a name (e.g. PolyFut), and submit.
  3. Copy the key — it usually starts with gsk_.
  4. Paste it below and click Save & continue.

You can skip and add a key later on the Match Analysis page. Open How to Use → AI Report anytime for the walkthrough with screenshots — and for what PolyFut sends to its AI service and how to turn that off.
